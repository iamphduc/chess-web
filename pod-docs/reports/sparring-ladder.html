At a glance

Goal. Make the app a place to practise for beating GM Le Quang Liem. The human said "Liem 1400" made no sense, and that the 1400 step felt "too solid, I don't feel any mistake". A measurement on 2026-10-17 backed that up: at 1400 he made 0.11 mistakes and 0.05 blunders per 40 moves, while real 1300–1500 players made 1.20 and 1.30.

What shipped.

  • Mistake and blunder bands in the move chooser, and a step table tuned so each sparring level makes about 75 % of human mistake and blunder rates.
  • npm run baseline: a dev-only script that fetched 150 analysed Lichess games per level and committed the human rates.
  • npm run calibrate -- --measure: each level plays itself from his book, and full-strength Stockfish grades its moves.
  • Sparring and Liem labels, the ♞ sparring card, and the page opening on vs Liem.

Key decisions

Red edge: the human made the call. Olive edge: an agent did, as a default the human can override. Entries are in pod-docs/decisions.md under the heading named on each card. No new design draft was made: the Look is the one from play-liem-look.html.

Liem is the final boss; the rest are sparring bots

Why: the project is practice for beating him, and "Liem 1400" made no sense.

Instead of: seven strengths of "Liem".

So: new labels at every level; Liem's level is today's Full.

human 2026-10-17, plan Key decisions

Every level plays his full book

Why: practising his lines is the point.

Instead of: leaving the book earlier at low levels (out of scope).

So: "Left his book" works in every review.

human plan Key decisions

Bots make about 75 % of real players' mistakes

Why: the real games are mostly blitz, and a 10-minute game should be cleaner.

Instead of: 100 % of the human rate.

So: TARGET_SHARE = 0.75, tolerance ±25 %, both table values.

human on the agent's recommendation

The page opens on vs Liem

Why: his ladder is the main screen.

Instead of: opening in Two players (now superseded).

So: two-player tests switch mode first; nothing loads Stockfish before Start game.

human · "The page opens on vs Liem"

Mistakes come from bands, with one extra draw only when eligible

Why: a pick from lines losing 20–30 win % (mistake) or 30+ (blunder) matches how the review grades. Drawing nothing when ineligible keeps Liem's picks the same, draw for draw.

Instead of: random moves from outside the top lines, or UCI_Elo (turned down on 2026-10-12).

So: multiPv rose to 10 so a band is rarely empty.

sprint planner sprint Shared contract

Baselines from real analysed Lichess games

Why: the 2026-10-17 measurement showed the review's formulas match Lichess's own counts.

Instead of: guessed rates, or fetching at run time.

So: a dev-only script and a committed JSON file. 2300 and 2500 lean on arena players' opponents.

default · "2300 and 2500 baselines include arena players' opponents"

The tuned table

Why: 14 runs in about 4 h brought all 12 rates within ±25 % of target.

Instead of: the 2026-10-12 table and its "1400 plays like 1400" check (both superseded).

So: 2300 and 2500 share their chances and differ by depth. Bots hang pieces more often than humans; that rate has no target.

T1 · "Sparring step table tuned to 75 % of human mistake rates"

Every text a level shows lives in levelView.ts

Why: one place to change a word, and one test file to check it.

Instead of: labels spread through the components.

So: components add no words of their own.

default · "Sparring and Liem labels"

No star by his name on the in-game card

Why: U1's ★ cut "Le Quang …" at 375 px, and main's card never had one.

Instead of: hiding the GM tag on phones, or showing "Liem" as the phone name (left for the human).

So: the name still ends "Le Quang Li…" at 375 px, as before.

Why: walking into a mate he sees on purpose would feel like throwing the game.

Instead of: real players' mate blindness.

So: a mated line is never in a band.

Review and fix pass

The reviewer read each wave's diff in full. It ran the verification, the measure mode, a small live npm run baseline, and the browser rows at 1400 px and 375 px. Its verdict, in its own words: "Plan review (round 1): Verdict: pass. No FIX findings." It made two PENDING findings:

  • The docs still described a gold star by his name on the in-game card. The orchestrator fixed this in the close-out commit.
  • npm run baseline -- --levels 1400 rewrites the baselines file with only that level. Queued as Fix next.

It dismissed six suspicions. Among them, it confirmed that the loader's expected exports match PR #75's gameAccuracy, lichessWinPercent and LICHESS_START_WIN. With no FIX findings, there was no Review fixes PR.
