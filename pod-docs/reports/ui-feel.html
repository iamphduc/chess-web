At a glance

Goal. A third polish round, from the human's own look at ui-motion. The human said (2026-10-21):

  • "The review speed feel really good but normal pieces feel too fast, maybe because of the transition type? Can you check chess.com to see how they do it?"
  • "Change the rounded avatar to square, review the gap in the player row too"
  • "For the two players, use a button instead of text button, I barely see it"

What shipped.

  • A game-board move now takes 200 ms with an ease-in-out curve, so the piece starts gently. Before, it took 150 ms with an ease-out that jumped about 20 % of the way in the first frame. The review's one-step playback keeps 150 ms ease-out.
  • Every avatar (♔, ♚, his ♞ and Liem's photo) is a 40 px square with 4 px corners.
  • The player row has more room (draft option C): 12 px from the avatar to the name, 16 px from the name side to the clock, and 8 px above and below. Each card is 60 px tall instead of 56 px.
  • On the home page, Two players is a full-width, 48 px outlined button under Play. Before, it was a small text link.
  • Cleanup: ReviewBoard's gameId and ply props are now required. This closes a "Fix next" queue entry from ui-motion.

The measurement behind the motion change. On 2026-10-21, someone sampled an e2–e4 move every frame with chrome-devtools-axi, as the plan's ## Why records. The plan doesn't name the agent. chess.com's analysis board at its default ("Medium") takes about 155 ms with an ease-in-out curve, covering 6–10 % of the way after the first frames. Ours took about 125–150 ms with an ease-out. Lichess's board library (chessground) and chessboard.js both default to 200 ms.

Key decisions

Game moves: 200 ms ease-in-out. The review keeps 150 ms ease-out.

Why
The old ease-out covered about 20 % of the way in the first frame, so the piece "leapt off its square". The ease-in-out curve covers under 5 % in that frame, reaches halfway at half time, and never overshoots.
Alternatives
Turned down: a speed setting (like chess.com's Slow / Medium / Fast) and springs.
Consequence
There are now two named transitions instead of one shared one. This supersedes the game-board part of the 2026-10-20 "Pieces move in 150 ms with an ease-out" entry.
Who
human accepted it, based on the chess.com measurement.
Links
decisions.md → "Game-board pieces move in 200 ms with an ease-in-out"

Avatars are squares with the 4 px corner

Why
The human asked for it: "Change the rounded avatar to square".
Alternatives
Keep circles. The plan's Look rules them out.
Consequence
The disc and the photo both use var(--radius). This supersedes the circle in "A color disc in place of a photo", 2026-10-20, but the disc itself stays.

Inside wave 1

Both slices wrote their tests first (350f11b, 4b371e7). M1's slice log says its wiring test "pinned today's behavior; each site broken by hand to prove it can fail". Each slice's NOTE in #100 is worth keeping:

  • M1: "codebase-structure.md now places .board > .player in Player.css at 60 px."
  • R1: "the clock hand's transform-origin: 50% 50% and a clip-path: inset(50%) stay; the test checks only corners." The contract said "No 50% is left on either" avatar, and the test first banned every 50% in the file. R1 narrowed it to corners (05b9a2c), so the clock hand's two non-corner 50% values stay.

agent-browser ran through plain Bash calls in both slices and in the wave check, and it didn't hang. This was its first plan back after the switch.

Review

The reviewer ran npm ci, the build and the full suite. In the browser, it sampled moves, the review, the Flip, drag drops, reduced motion, the player row at five widths, and home. It dismissed four concerns after checking each one:

  • Home doesn't import Button.css itself. Dropped, because "the app is one bundle with no lazy routes", and on a fresh load at #/ the button had its outline.
  • The 60 px cards could shrink the board. Dropped: .squares measured 624, 416 and 312 px at 1400, 992 and 375 px.
  • A 200 ms move could leave a piece off its square when his reply lands mid-move. Dropped: "his reply landed 35 to 56 ms after my click four times, and 300 ms later every piece sat inside its [data-square] box."
  • tests/motion-wiring.test.ts reads the source, so it could be hollow. Dropped, because "it checks the call order inside the drop and Flip handlers", and the runtime checks agree with it.
Where the human stepped in: the three requests above, the draft picks ("1C 2B", with C chosen over the recommended B), accepting the 200 ms ease-in-out curve, the merge target, and the switch back to agent-browser. All of these came before the run. The files record no human answer during the run.
