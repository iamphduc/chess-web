At a glance

Goal (from the plan): "Give the app a front door and one consistent look before calling it finished." The human's own words drove it: the review graph was "kinda far away from the analysis sideboard", they wanted "a home page to start first, not just straight to the board", and they saw "some inconsistency in the gap".

What shipped:

  • A home page at #/ with a level picker, your color, Play and a Two players link.
  • Hash screens #/play, #/two-players and #/review, with working Back and Forward.
  • Level names (Easy … Master, ★ Liem) in place of ratings on the sparring levels.
  • A board screen with a top bar, one Moves card and a "+N" material line. The tabs, the challenge card and Fallen Pieces are gone.
  • The review's win-% graph at the top of the sidebar, or of the phone sheet.
  • One light "scorebook" look in Quicksand on every screen. The old dark tokens are deleted, and a test guards against them.

Key decisions

The planning interview settled the big ones. The engineers set the smaller defaults, and C1 wrote them all to decisions.md. The design draft is ui-refresh-look.html.

Home page first, with hash links

human
Why
"a home page to start first". Hash links work on GitHub Pages with no server.
Alternatives
Keep opening on vs Liem (the 2026-10-18 entry), or use a router library.
Consequence
A reload of #/play or #/review goes home, because the store isn't saved.

decisions.md entry

Going home ends the game

agent default
Why
← Home, Back and an edited hash all reach home mid-game, and one rule covers all three.
Alternatives
Keep the game and let #/play resume it.
Consequence
There's no "resume game", and the engine reloads (from cache) on the next game.

decisions.md entry

Names, not ratings

human names: default
Why
A 1000-rated player who beats "1400" easily would think the app is wrong.
Alternatives
Keep "Sparring · N" and "about N" (sparring-ladder's labels).
Consequence
The numbers live on in code and npm run calibrate. Only Liem shows "GM · 2732".

decisions.md entry

Look B in Quicksand, light only

human light only: default
Why
"I like the classic look of the B score book but I don't like the serif font so can we change it to something like the A font."
Alternatives
Looks A and C in the draft, and a dark mode.
Consequence
Every color is a token in src/index.css. No other CSS file names a color.

decisions.md entry · grade colors · ochre highlight

Top bar and one Moves card

human
Why
The tabs, the buttons box and Fallen Pieces made the gaps uneven.
Alternatives
Restyle the old panels in place.
Consequence
Fallen Pieces became "+N" on the cards. Review game is the first item in the foot (default).

decisions.md entry · Review game · "+N" counts promotions

New game replays the level and color

agent default
Why
With the setup card gone, New game needed a meaning.
Alternatives
Keep the drawn color when the pick is Random.
Consequence
← Home is the only way to change the level or color.

decisions.md entry

The graph in the sidebar, shown once

human phone rules: default
Why
The graph sat "kinda far away from the analysis sideboard".
Alternatives
The full-width strip above the board, with the White / You switch.
Consequence
graphPlacement picks the sheet or the sidebar, never both. The graph always shows your side.

decisions.md entry · Play again and Back to game

Merge into pod-staging

human
Why
So that unattended code never deploys on its own.
Alternatives
Merge straight into main, which deploys.
Consequence
The human looks at pod-staging, then moves it to main.

How the agents worked together

The orchestrator ran under autopilot. It drafted the sprint from the plan, dispatched one engineer per slice in each wave (all on opus), merged the slice branches into a wave branch, ran a Look check in the browser, and opened the wave PR. Every slice came back at medium confidence with "time lost none".
